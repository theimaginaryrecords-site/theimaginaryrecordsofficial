Live Music in India: The Growing Concert Economy

India is in the middle of a live music boom. Stadiums are selling out, international superstars are adding Indian dates, and concerts have become economic events that move entire cities. For artists, the live circuit is no longer a side income — it is becoming the centre of the business.

The numbers tell the story

According to an EY socio-economic report, Diljit Dosanjh's Dil-Luminati India tour — 14 shows across 13 cities between October and December 2024 — sold over 320,000 tickets and generated an estimated economic impact of Rs 943 crore (about $103 million). The tour supported more than 118,000 man-days of employment across production, security, logistics, transport, and hospitality. Coldplay's Ahmedabad concerts, covered in an EY-Parthenon and BookMyShow Live report, generated an estimated economic impact of Rs 641 crore, drawing about 222,000 fans from more than 500 cities across all 28 states and five union territories.

Concert tourism is real

Fans are travelling for shows. The EY report on the Dil-Luminati tour found that about 38% of attendees travelled from other cities, with more than half extending their stay beyond the event dates — boosting hotels, food, transport, and shopping. Nearly half the audience came from Tier II and Tier III cities, showing that large live events now reach far beyond the metros. Ticket sales accounted for about 80% of direct revenues, and all tickets were purchased through digital platforms.

What this means for independent artists

The boom is not only for stadium acts. A growing concert economy means more venues, more promoters, more sound and production crews, and more opening slots. Artists who build a live show — even at the club and college level — position themselves to ride the wave. Live performance also converts casual listeners into loyal fans faster than almost anything else, and it pays in ways streaming alone rarely does.

How to enter the live circuit

Start by performing wherever you can: college fests, local venues, opening slots for bigger acts, and community events. Build a tight live set, film it well, and use those videos to pitch for bigger shows. Promoters book artists who can draw a crowd and deliver a professional show — and the only way to prove both is to perform. As India's concert economy grows, the artists with live experience will be the ones who get the calls.
