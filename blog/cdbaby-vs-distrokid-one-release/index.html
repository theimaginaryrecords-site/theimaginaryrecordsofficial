CD Baby vs DistroKid: Which Is Cheaper for One Single? (2026 Math)

The Imaginary Records · Distributor Guides · 6 min read

Releasing one song this year and nothing else? The "cheapest" distributor depends entirely on your time horizon. Let's do the actual math.

Year 1 costs

  • CD Baby: $9.99 one-time. You keep 91% forever. Total year 1: $9.99.
  • DistroKid: $24.99/year. You keep 100%. Total year 1: $24.99 (+$4.95 if you want Content ID).

CD Baby wins year 1 by a mile.

Year 3 costs (same single, still earning)

  • CD Baby: still $9.99 total. 91% royalties.
  • DistroKid: $24.99 × 3 = $74.97 — or your song gets taken down. Add Leave a Legacy ($29) to protect it: $103.97.

For a single release with a long tail, CD Baby costs 90% less over three years.

When DistroKid wins

Release 10+ songs a year and DistroKid's unlimited model crushes per-release pricing: $24.99/year for everything vs $99.90+ on CD Baby. The break-even is around 3 singles per year.

The royalty difference

CD Baby keeps 9% forever; DistroKid gives 100% while subscribed. On a song earning $1,000/year: CD Baby costs you $90/year in commission; DistroKid costs $24.99/year in fees. Above ~$280/year in earnings, DistroKid's 100% beats CD Baby's 91% — if you keep paying the subscription.

The rule of thumb

  • 1–2 releases/year, want them up forever: CD Baby
  • 5+ releases/year: DistroKid (or TuneCore/Ditto)
  • High-earning catalog: 100%-royalty subscription, but budget for Leave a Legacy
  • Indian artist, INR payouts, no yearly fee: TIR at upto 85%
