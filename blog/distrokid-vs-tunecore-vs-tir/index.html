DistroKid vs TuneCore vs TIR: Honest Comparison in Rupees (2026)

If you're an Indian artist choosing a distributor, you've probably narrowed it down to the two big American names — DistroKid and TuneCore — and maybe wondered how a homegrown option like The Imaginary Records compares. Here's the honest math, in rupees, with no sales pitch.

The pricing models, side by side

  • DistroKid: ~$22.99/year for the basic Musician plan (roughly ₹1,900/year at current rates), unlimited uploads. You keep 100% of royalties. But you pay every year whether you earn or not — stop paying and your music can come down.
  • TuneCore: ~$14.99/year per release on the Rising Artist plan (roughly ₹1,250/year per single or album). You keep 100% of royalties. Five singles out? That's ~₹6,250/year in fees.
  • TIR: No joining fee, no yearly fee, unlimited releases. You keep 85% of royalties; TIR retains 15% as its fee.

The break-even math nobody shows you

Assume an average of ₹0.15 per stream. With DistroKid's ₹1,900/year fee, TIR's 15% cut costs you less until you're earning serious numbers:

  • At 10,000 streams/year (₹1,500 royalties): DistroKid costs ₹1,900 in fees — you lose money. TIR costs ₹225 (15%) — you keep ₹1,275.
  • At 50,000 streams/year (₹7,500): DistroKid ₹1,900 vs TIR ₹1,125. TIR still cheaper.
  • At 100,000 streams/year (₹15,000): DistroKid ₹1,900 vs TIR ₹2,250. Now DistroKid edges ahead.

The crossover sits around 85,000 streams per year. Below that — where the vast majority of independent artists are — the no-fee percentage model costs you less. Above it, flat-fee services win. Be honest about where you are.

Beyond price: what actually differs

  • Payment: DistroKid and TuneCore pay in USD via PayPal or bank transfer with currency conversion. TIR pays in rupees to Indian bank accounts — no forex loss, no PayPal fees.
  • Indian platforms: JioSaavn, Gaana, Wynk and Hungama matter enormously for Indian artists. An India-based distributor has direct relationships and faster issue resolution with these platforms.
  • Support: Email a US distributor and wait. With TIR, you're talking to a team in your timezone that answers in your language.
  • Content ID and extras: DistroKid charges extra for some features (leave-a-legacy fees, etc.). TIR includes Content ID enrollment and ISRC/UPC codes in the free distribution.

When to choose which

Choose DistroKid or TuneCore if you're consistently doing 100k+ streams a year and want to keep 100% — the math favors you. Choose TIR if you're building, releasing frequently, want zero upfront cost, earn in rupees, or value having a distributor that actually picks up the phone. Many artists use both: TIR for Indian-market releases, a US service for international catalogs. There's no rule against it.
