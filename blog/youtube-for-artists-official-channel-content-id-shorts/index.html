YouTube for Artists: Official Artist Channel, Content ID, and Shorts

YouTube is two platforms in one for musicians: a video site and a streaming service. The Official Artist Channel, Content ID, and Shorts are the three systems that tie them together. Understanding each one is the difference between your music working for you on YouTube and your music floating around unclaimed.

Official Artist Channel (OAC)

An Official Artist Channel is a single verified channel that brings together your official uploads, your auto-generated Topic content, your Shorts, and your releases. It carries a music note badge next to your name and gives you artist-specific shelves and a discography section. To be eligible, you must own and operate a channel focused on one artist or band, comply with YouTube's policies, have at least one official music release on YouTube delivered by a distributor or label, and have a Topic channel with your name. You also need at least one of the following: YouTube Partner Program membership, a YouTube Partner Manager, a label network that works with a Partner Manager, or distribution through a music partner listed in YouTube's Services Directory. Most independent artists get their OAC through their distributor, which requests it on their behalf.

Content ID

Content ID is YouTube's automated rights-management system. It scans every uploaded video against a database of reference audio and video files. When someone uploads a video using your music and it matches your reference file, you as the rights holder can choose to monetize, track, or block that video. Your distributor usually registers your releases with Content ID for you. Without it, other people can use your music in videos and you will see none of the revenue.

Shorts

Shorts are YouTube's short-form vertical videos, and they are a major discovery surface for new artists. Since October 2024, vertical or square videos up to three minutes long count as Shorts. Most songs from the Shorts audio library can cover up to 90 seconds of a three-minute Short, and some tracks are limited to 60 or 30 seconds. Shorts using your music can also earn through YouTube's Shorts Music Revenue Sharing pool. Use Shorts to post hooks, behind-the-scenes clips, and performance teasers that point viewers toward your full songs.

Set up all three systems before your next release, not after. An OAC, Content ID registration, and a Shorts plan turn YouTube from a place your music happens to appear into a channel you control.
