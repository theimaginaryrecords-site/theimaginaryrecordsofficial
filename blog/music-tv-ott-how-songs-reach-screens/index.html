Music on TV and OTT: How Songs Reach Screens

A single placement in a hit show can change an artist's career overnight. When Kate Bush's 1985 song Running Up That Hill appeared in Netflix's Stranger Things, it saw a reported 9,900% uptick in US Spotify streams and reached No. 3 on the Billboard Hot 100 — 37 years after its release. That is the power of sync. Here is how songs reach TV and OTT screens.

What sync licensing is

Sync licensing means licensing a song for use with visual media — films, TV shows, web series, ads, games, trailers, and documentaries. Two rights are needed: the master (the recording) and the publishing (the composition). A music supervisor — the person who chooses songs for a production — needs both cleared before a song can be used. A split sheet signed in the room, a registered work, and an identified publisher and master owner who answers emails are not admin — they are the product.

How much sync pays

No trade body publishes a verified rate card for sync. The most cited public figures come from practitioner Ari Herstand, who reports typical upfront fees for independent artists running roughly $500 to $20,000 for television, $10,000 to $80,000 for films and trailers, $2,000 to $10,000 for video games, and $20,000 into the high six figures for commercial campaigns. These are practitioner estimates, not audited data. One independent musician reported receiving $1,272 for publishing plus $1,272 for mechanical royalties from a Netflix placement.

Sync in India

India's sync ecosystem is still maturing. Production houses working on OTT content often prefer to commission new music rather than license existing songs, partly because of pre-existing relationships with composers, and legacy labels have guarded their catalogues. But the pieces are coming together: publishers are pitching more systematically, organisations like IPRS are working to streamline licensing and royalty collection, and marketplaces like Hoopr connect independent artists with brands and productions. One sync marketplace executive expects the sync market for Indian independent artists to triple in the next two years.

How to get your music placed

Music supervisors source songs through publishers, sync agents, licensing marketplaces, and direct pitches. Platforms like SYNCR run briefs for real productions — including Netflix and Apple TV+ series — where artists can submit tracks. The keys: own your rights, keep metadata accurate, register your works, and make your songs easy to clear. A supervisor on a deadline picks the song that is ready, not the song that is best.

Why sync matters for independents

Sync pays upfront, pays royalties, and exposes music to audiences who never visit streaming apps. As one music supervisor put it, he has placed songs with 100 views on YouTube that suddenly had hundreds of thousands or millions of views after sync. For independent artists, a placement can lead to tours, distribution deals, and label interest. Build the catalogue, keep it clearable, and pitch it.
