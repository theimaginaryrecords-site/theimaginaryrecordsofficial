How Record Labels Work: What Labels Actually Do for Artists

Many artists dream of getting signed without fully understanding what a label actually does. A record label is a company that turns creative work into commercial releases — handling everything from recording and distribution to marketing and copyright protection. Here is how the machine really works.

A&R: Finding and Developing Talent

It starts with A&R — Artists and Repertoire. The A&R division scouts new talent, signs artists, and oversees their artistic development. A&R executives attend live shows, listen to demos, and track rising artists online. Once an artist is signed, A&R helps select songs, match the artist with producers, and shape the sound of a release. The term "record label" itself comes from the circular label in the centre of a vinyl record, which displayed the manufacturer's name.

Production Support

Labels provide access to professional studios, producers, mixing engineers, and session musicians. For a new artist, this can be the difference between a bedroom demo and a radio-ready record. The label typically funds the recording costs upfront — though in most traditional deals, those costs are recouped from the artist's future earnings before royalties flow.

Distribution: Getting Music Everywhere

Distribution is the label's core logistical job: getting music onto streaming services like Spotify and Apple Music, into physical stores, and across international markets. Major labels own distribution networks that can put a release in front of millions of listeners within days. Independent labels and distributors offer the same essential service, often with more flexibility and transparency.

Marketing and Promotion

Marketing is one of a label's most important functions. Labels craft campaigns, secure playlist placements, run PR, arrange media coverage, and build an artist's public image. They also employ publicists who help performers gain positive media coverage, and arrange for merchandise to be available through stores and other outlets.

Publishing, Sync, and Copyright

Labels also handle music publishing and copyright enforcement — protecting the recordings, licensing music for TV, film, and advertising (known as sync), and collecting royalties. They may help book shows and tours, and in broader deals, participate in merchandise and other revenue. In short, a label is a full business partner: it invests money, expertise, and connections in exchange for a share of the returns. The key question for any artist is whether that trade is worth it — which depends entirely on the deal.
