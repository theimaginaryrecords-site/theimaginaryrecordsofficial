The Beatles: How Four Musicians From Liverpool Changed Music Forever

The Beatles were an English rock band formed in Liverpool in 1960, consisting of John Lennon, Paul McCartney, George Harrison, and Ringo Starr. Emerging from Liverpool's thriving beat scene, the group became an international phenomenon — and went on to reshape songwriting, recording, album-making, and popular culture itself.

Liverpool Beginnings and Beatlemania

The band developed from John Lennon's earlier group, The Quarrymen, and exploded into Beatlemania in the early 1960s on singles such as "Love Me Do," "She Loves You," and "I Want to Hold Your Hand." Their February 1964 appearance on The Ed Sullivan Show became a defining moment of Beatlemania and the British Invasion of the United States. They achieved 17 number-one singles on the UK Singles Chart during their original career, including "Hey Jude," "Help!," and "All You Need Is Love." They also made their film debut with A Hard Day's Night (1964), followed by Help! (1965).

The Studio Years

A growing desire to refine their studio work, along with the strain of concert touring, led the Beatles to retire from live performances in 1966. Working extensively with producer George Martin, they rapidly expanded the possibilities of popular music through ambitious songwriting, arrangements, and studio experimentation. Albums including Rubber Soul (1965), Revolver (1966), and Sgt. Pepper's Lonely Hearts Club Band (1967) became landmarks in rock history. The success of these records heralded the album era and furthered advancements in electronic music, album art, and music videos. Sgt. Pepper's was later inducted into the Grammy Hall of Fame in 1993 and chosen by the Library of Congress for the National Recording Registry in 2003.

The Final Albums and Breakup

The band enjoyed further commercial success with The Beatles (also known as "the White Album," 1968) and Abbey Road (1969), and founded Apple Corps in 1968, a multimedia corporation that continues to oversee projects related to their legacy. After the Beatles' break-up in 1970, all four members enjoyed success as solo artists.

Legacy

Lennon was murdered in 1980 and Harrison died of lung cancer in 2001; McCartney and Starr remain musically active. With more than 600 million records sold, the Beatles turned the album into an art form, made the recording studio an instrument in its own right, and set the template that almost every band since has followed.
