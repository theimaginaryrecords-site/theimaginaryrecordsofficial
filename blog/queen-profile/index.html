Queen: Freddie Mercury and the Anthems That Never Die

By Aabid Khan · October 14, 2026

Queen: Freddie Mercury and the Anthems That Never Die

Queen are a British rock band formed in London in 1970 by Freddie Mercury, Brian May, and Roger Taylor, with bassist John Deacon joining the following year. Emerging from Britain's early-1970s rock scene, the group developed an ambitious sound combining hard rock, progressive rock, glam, pop, and elaborate vocal arrangements — and produced some of the most enduring songs ever recorded.

Freddie Mercury: The Frontman

Freddie Mercury was born Farrokh Bulsara on September 5, 1946, in Zanzibar, to Parsi-Indian parents. He attended British-style boarding schools in India from the age of eight, and in 1964 his family fled the Zanzibar Revolution, moving to Middlesex, England. He formed Queen in 1970 with May and Taylor. Regarded as one of the greatest singers in the history of rock music, he was known for his flamboyant stage persona and four-octave vocal range.

The Breakthrough: Bohemian Rhapsody

Queen's international breakthrough came with A Night at the Opera (1975), featuring "Bohemian Rhapsody" — a six-minute suite blending ballad, opera, and hard rock that topped the UK charts for nine weeks. The song later reached number one on the UK Singles Chart a second time, following Mercury's death in 1991. Mercury wrote numerous hits for Queen, including "Killer Queen," "Somebody to Love," "We Are the Champions," "Don't Stop Me Now," and "Crazy Little Thing Called Love." The band's early run had begun with their self-titled debut album in 1973, followed by Queen II, and Mercury also pursued a solo career, releasing the album Mr. Bad Guy in 1984.

Live Aid 1985

Queen's performance at Live Aid at Wembley Stadium in 1985 became one of the most celebrated live sets in rock history, with Mercury commanding the vast crowd through call-and-response and sheer stage presence. The band's catalogue also includes stadium staples "We Will Rock You" and "Another One Bites the Dust."

Legacy

Mercury died on November 24, 1991, at the age of 45, and was posthumously featured on the band's final album, Made in Heaven (1995). A tribute concert was held at Wembley Stadium in 1992 in benefit of AIDS awareness. The band's career was dramatised in the 2018 biopic Bohemian Rhapsody, and May and Taylor later continued Queen's legacy, touring extensively with singer Adam Lambert.
