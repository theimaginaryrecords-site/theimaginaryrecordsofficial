The Vinyl Revival: Why Records Are Selling Again

By Aabid Khan · October 14, 2026

The Vinyl Revival: Why Records Are Selling Again

Vinyl was supposed to be dead. Streaming made every song in history available for a monthly fee, and the CD had already pushed records to the margins decades ago. Yet vinyl is now enjoying one of the longest growth runs in music history — and it shows no sign of stopping.

The numbers behind the comeback

According to the Recording Industry Association of America (RIAA), US vinyl sales reached $1.04 billion in 2025, the first time the format crossed the $1 billion mark since 1983. Unit sales hit 46.8 million, up 7.9% from 43.4 million in 2024. That made it the nineteenth consecutive year of vinyl growth in the US. Vinyl now accounts for roughly three-quarters of all US physical-format revenue, while CD revenue fell to $312.4 million.

Why fans are buying records again

The revival is driven by more than nostalgia. A vinyl record is a physical object in a world where music has become invisible — something to hold, display, and collect. Album artwork gets its full scale back, and the ritual of putting on a record makes listening deliberate rather than background noise. For many fans, buying vinyl is also a way to support an artist directly in a way that a stream never can.

Superstars and collectors fuel the boom

Big artists have leaned into the format. Taylor Swift's "The Life of a Showgirl" was 2025's best-selling vinyl album in the US with about 1.6 million copies, released in eight vinyl variants including a Target exclusive. Sabrina Carpenter and Kendrick Lamar also posted strong vinyl numbers, while classics like Fleetwood Mac's "Rumours" kept selling alongside new releases. Collectible variants, coloured pressings, and limited editions have turned vinyl into a merch category of its own.

What it means for independent artists

Vinyl is not just for superstars. For independent artists, a small vinyl run can be a powerful tool: a premium product for your most loyal fans, a merch-table item with far better margins than a stream, and a physical artefact that makes your music feel real. Pressing costs and minimum orders mean it takes planning, but for artists with an engaged audience, vinyl is one of the most rewarding formats available today.

The lesson for artists is simple: physical formats and streaming are not enemies. They serve different needs. Streaming is discovery — the way new listeners find you. Vinyl is commitment — the way your most dedicated fans show their support and deepen their connection to your music. Smart artists use both, letting streaming bring people in and physical products give them something to hold onto.
