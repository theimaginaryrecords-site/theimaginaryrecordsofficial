Sonu Nigam: Three Decades of India's Most Versatile Voice

Sonu Nigam, born 30 July 1973 in Faridabad, Haryana, is one of Indian music's most enduring playback singers. He began performing on stage with his father, Agam Kumar Nigam, at the age of four. Across a career spanning more than three decades, he has recorded over 6,000 songs in more than 32 languages, moving between romantic ballads, classical pieces, devotional songs, and pop hits. In 2023, he received the Padma Shri, India's fourth-highest civilian award.

Early Years and First Break

Nigam moved to Mumbai as a teenager to pursue singing. His first film song, for the unreleased movie Janam in 1990, did not bring immediate recognition. His breakthrough came with "Accha Sila Diya" from the 1995 film Bewafa Sanam. Around the same time, he hosted the television show Sa Re Ga Ma Pa from 1995 to 1999, which made him a household name across India.

The Songs That Made Him a Star

The late 1990s established Nigam as a leading playback voice, with songs like "Sandese Aate Hai" from Border (1997) and "Yeh Dil Deewana" from Pardes (1997). His status was firmly sealed with the title track of Kal Ho Naa Ho in 2003, a performance that earned him a National Film Award and a Filmfare Award. Later highlights include "Suraj Hua Maddham" from Kabhi Khushi Kabhie Gham, "Saathiya" from Saathiya, "Main Agar Kahoon" from Om Shanti Om, and "Abhi Mujh Mein Kahin" from Agneepath (2012).

Beyond Playback Singing

Nigam has also worked as a music director, television host, and judge, mentoring new talent on shows such as Indian Idol and X Factor India. His album Deewana (1999) became a major pop success. In 2013, he ranked number one on the US Billboard Uncharted charts, and in 2014 he received an Oscar Academy Awards nomination for the soundtrack of Jal. In 2020, he launched his own label, I Believe Music.

Legacy

Often compared to the legendary Mohammed Rafi, whom he admired deeply, Nigam is regarded as one of the finest technical singers of his generation. His ability to adapt across genres, languages, and eras has kept him relevant from the cassette era to the streaming age, and his devotional songs and classical-based performances remain a core part of his concerts and recordings.
