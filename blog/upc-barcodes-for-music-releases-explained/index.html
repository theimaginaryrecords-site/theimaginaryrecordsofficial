UPC Barcodes for Music Releases Explained

By Aabid Khan · October 14, 2026

UPC Barcodes for Music Releases Explained

A UPC is the 12-digit Universal Product Code that identifies your release as a product. If the ISRC is your recording's ID, the UPC is your release's ID. An album with ten songs has ten ISRCs and one UPC. Every release needs one, and understanding how UPCs work prevents some of the most confusing catalogue mistakes independent artists make.

What a UPC actually identifies

A UPC identifies one release: the single, EP, or album that a store lists and a customer buys or streams. Different versions of a release need different UPCs, because each version is a different product. A remaster, a deluxe edition, and the original album are three products, so they get three UPCs. The same logic applies across formats: if you already released a song physically, you need a different UPC for the digital release, because the physical and digital products are separate. You can use the same ISRCs across both, since the recordings are the same.

How to get a UPC

Most artists never need to buy a UPC themselves. Your distributor assigns one automatically when you submit a release, and it is included as part of the distribution service. GS1 is the global organisation that administers UPC codes, and artists who want their own codes can register with GS1 to obtain a company prefix. GS1 charges registration and annual fees, so check GS1's current pricing before going that route. What you should never do is invent a UPC yourself. A UPC only works as a unique identifier if it is assigned properly through a legitimate source.

Why UPCs matter for digital releases

  • Sales tracking: stores and charts track your release's performance through its UPC.
  • Royalty collection: correct UPC coding helps attribute sales and streams to the right artist and rights holders.
  • Catalogue clarity: distinct UPCs keep different versions of your releases from being confused with each other.
  • Physical inventory: for CDs and vinyl, the barcode on the packaging is generated from the UPC.

Keep a record of every UPC alongside its release title, format, and version. When you move distributors, hand over your existing UPCs and ISRCs so your catalogue history stays intact.

If you are releasing physically, remember the barcode on the packaging comes from the UPC, so the code must be final before you send artwork to print. A changed UPC after printing means reprinted packaging, which is an expensive and avoidable mistake.
