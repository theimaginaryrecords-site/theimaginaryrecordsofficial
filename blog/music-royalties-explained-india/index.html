Music Royalties Explained: How Indian Artists Actually Earn (2026)

The Imaginary Records · Royalties · 9 min read

"How do I get paid for my music?" is the most common question we hear from new artists. The royalty system looks complicated, but it breaks down into a few clear income streams. Here's the full picture for Indian independent artists.

1. Streaming royalties (master)

Every time someone streams your song on Spotify, JioSaavn, Apple Music, or YouTube Music, the platform pays a small per-stream amount to whoever owns the recording — usually you, via your distributor. Rates vary by platform, country, and whether the listener is on free or premium. These add up: 100,000 streams across platforms can mean meaningful income.

2. Publishing royalties (songwriting)

Separate from the recording, the composition (melody + lyrics) earns its own royalties whenever it's streamed, performed, or reproduced. In India, IPRS (Indian Performing Right Society) collects these for songwriters. If you wrote your song, register with IPRS — it's a second income stream most indie artists miss.

3. YouTube Content ID

YouTube's system scans every upload for your music. When someone uses your song in their video or Short, you can monetize it — ads run, and you earn. This is often an Indian artist's biggest surprise income source, because covers, dance videos, and reels using your audio all generate claims.

4. Sync licensing

Films, web series, ads, and games pay to use music. Sync fees range from a few thousand rupees for small projects to lakhs for major placements. Having clean metadata and instrumental versions ready makes your catalog sync-friendly.

5. Live performance royalties

When your songs are performed publicly — concerts, radio, cafes — IPRS and PPL collect license fees from venues and broadcasters and distribute them to rights holders.

How the money reaches you

The chain looks like this: platform → distributor → you (master royalties), and society → you (publishing). Your distributor aggregates earnings from 200+ platforms into one dashboard and pays out on schedule. With TIR, you keep upto 85% of your royalties — one of the highest shares available to Indian independents.

What to watch out for

  • Unclaimed royalties: register your works with IPRS so publishing income doesn't sit unclaimed.
  • Splits: agree in writing who owns what percentage before release — producer, co-writers, featured artists.
  • Statements: your distributor should show per-platform, per-track earnings. If they don't, ask why.
