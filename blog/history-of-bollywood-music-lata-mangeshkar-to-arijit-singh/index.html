The History of Bollywood Music: From Lata Mangeshkar to Arijit Singh

Few film industries in the world are as inseparable from their music as Bollywood. Hindi film songs are not background decoration — they are the emotional centre of the films themselves, and they have shaped Indian popular culture for nearly a century. This is the story of how Bollywood music evolved, era by era.

Playback is born

Before playback, actors sang their own songs on set. That changed in 1935, when the first playback song was recorded for Nitin Bose's film Dhoop Chhaon by music directors R.C. Boral and Pankaj Mullick. Playback — recording songs separately in advance, then filming actors lip-syncing to them — separated the production of music from the production of films, and gave Hindi film songs an independent life of their own. Songs were released before films to promote them, a practice that continues today.

The golden age

1947 was a turning point: singing star K.L. Saigal died early that year, and the reigning female singing star Noor Jehan moved to the newly created Pakistan, leaving a huge void. It was filled by Lata Mangeshkar and Mohammed Rafi, who went on to become playback legends. In 1949, the film Mahal featured Lata's "Aayega Aanewala," initially credited on the record to the on-screen character rather than the singer. Fans demanded to know the real singer's name, and from then on, crediting playback singers became the norm. The late 1940s and 1950s are generally regarded as the Golden Age of Hindi film music, with composers like Naushad and S.D. Burman, lyricists like Shakeel Badayuni and Shailendra, and voices including Mukesh, Talat Mahmood, and Geeta Dutt.

Decade by decade

Each era brought new defining voices. The 1960s were dominated by Lata Mangeshkar, Mohammed Rafi, and Mukesh; the 1970s by Asha Bhosle, Kishore Kumar, and Manna Dey; the 1980s by singers like Shailendra Singh, Shabbir Kumar, and Anuradha Paudwal; and the 1990s by Udit Narayan, Kumar Sanu, and Alka Yagnik. The 2000s saw pop-circuit singers like Shaan and KK (Krishnakumar Kunnath) enter films, alongside reality-show discoveries like Shreya Ghoshal and Sunidhi Chauhan.

The Arijit era — and beyond film music

Today, Arijit Singh is the defining playback voice of his generation. But the landscape has shifted: the pandemic accelerated a trend toward non-film singles, and streaming data shows that a large share of top Hindi songs are now singles, not film tracks. Independent artists like King, Darshan Raval, and AP Dhillon have built massive followings outside the film system. Bollywood music is no longer only film music — but the playback tradition that started in 1935 still runs through all of it.
