Recoupment Explained: Why Artists Can Earn Millions and Still Owe Money

Recoupment is the reason an artist can generate millions in revenue and receive nothing. It is the accounting mechanism by which a label recovers its investment — the advance, recording costs, video costs, tour support — out of the artist's share of royalties before the artist is paid. The label starts profiting from its own share from the first stream. The artist earns nothing until the recoupable balance hits zero. This is not a scam; it is the standard structure of traditional record deals. But artists who do not understand it sign deals that surprise them for years.

The core mechanic: recoupment comes out of YOUR share

This is the part that catches artists off guard. Recoupable costs do not come out of total revenue — they come out of the artist's royalty share only. Take a worked example: a release generates €100,000 in net receipts. Your royalty rate is 18%, so your share is €18,000 and the label's is €82,000. The label spent €50,000 on recording and marketing, all recoupable. That €50,000 comes out of your €18,000, not out of the €100,000. Your share goes to zero, and you are still €32,000 unrecouped. You earned nothing on a release that earned €100,000, while the label is already in profit on its share. That is the structure doing exactly what it was designed to do.

What counts as recoupable

Typical recoupable costs include the advance, recording costs, producer fees, artwork, mastering, manufacturing, distribution costs, tour support, and often 50% of music video costs. Marketing costs are sometimes borne by the label and sometimes recoupable — the contract decides. Typical artist royalty rates in traditional deals run around 15–20% of sales revenue. At those rates, an artist must generate five to six times the recoupable balance in label revenue before seeing a royalty check.

Cross-collateralisation: the hidden multiplier

Many contracts are cross-collateralised, meaning unrecouped costs from one album can be deducted from the earnings of another. A flop first album can swallow the royalties of a hit second album before you earn a cent from either. Cross-collateralisation is one of the most important clauses to negotiate — and one of the least discussed before signing.

Why so many artists stay unrecouped

Most new artists never recoup. Industry estimates commonly cited are that the large majority of signings never earn back their advances — roughly eight in ten, by some accounts. That does not mean the label lost money: as the example above shows, the label can profit on its share while the artist remains unrecouped. It means the artist's royalty account stayed in the red. If you sign a traditional deal, go in knowing the maths, not the headline advance — and read the recoupment clause before you read anything else.
