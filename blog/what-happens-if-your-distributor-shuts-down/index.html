What Happens If Your Distributor Shuts Down?

By Aabid Khan · October 14, 2026

What Happens If Your Distributor Shuts Down?

No distributor is too big to fail, and none is too small to stop caring about your catalogue. If your distributor shuts down, stops paying, or freezes your account, your music can disappear from stores and unpaid royalties can be put at risk. You cannot prevent a company from closing, but you can make sure your career survives it. Here is how.

What actually happens to your music

If a distributor closes, its delivery pipeline closes with it. Your releases can be removed from streaming platforms, new royalty reports stop arriving, and any balance sitting in your distributor account may become difficult or impossible to recover. Even short of a shutdown, warning signs include payout delays, unanswered support tickets, and account freezes. Distributors today also act as copyright enforcement gatekeepers — they can freeze revenue, reject releases, remove tracks, or terminate accounts over ownership disputes, AI-content questions, or suspected streaming fraud. Your distributor matters far more than it did when distribution was just plumbing.

Step 1: Keep your own copies of everything

Your best protection is independence from any single company. Keep your own copies of every master audio file, cover artwork file, ISRC code, UPC code, metadata sheet, songwriter split agreement, and licensing document. Owning the masters is not the same as controlling the catalogue — a switch is the moment that difference shows up. If your only copy of an ISRC lives inside a dashboard you can no longer reach, recovering your catalogue becomes far harder.

Step 2: Re-deliver through a new distributor

The recovery process is the same as a normal distributor switch: upload your catalogue to a new distributor using the same ISRCs and UPCs, identical audio, identical artwork, and identical metadata, wait for the platforms to link the new delivery to your existing release, and only then confirm the old version is gone. Reusing your ISRCs is what preserves your stream counts and playlist placements. Test the process with one release before moving the whole catalogue.

Step 3: Read the terms before you need them

Before choosing any distributor, read its terms on takedowns, royalty forfeiture, payout withholding, and what happens to your catalogue if you cancel. Policies differ sharply. DistroKid removes your catalogue when a subscription lapses unless you bought its Leave a Legacy add-on. Amuse keeps your music live but takes a 25% cut of ongoing royalties instead of pulling the release. Too Lost and LANDR keep releases earning at 85% after cancellation. A distributor with a clear, written continuity policy is worth more than a slightly lower fee.

How to reduce the risk upfront

Withdraw your royalties regularly instead of letting a large balance accumulate in a distributor account. Avoid keeping your entire income dependent on one company. Keep your metadata and identifiers backed up outside the platform. And favour distributors that are transparent about ownership, finances, and terms — if you cannot find clear answers about what happens to your money and your music in a worst case, treat that as a red flag.
