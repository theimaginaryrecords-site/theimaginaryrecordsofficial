Royalty Splits Guide: How to Divide Earnings Between Collaborators (2026)

Most collaboration disputes aren't about creativity — they're about money nobody agreed on upfront. Here's how royalty splits actually work across distributors, and how to set them up so nobody fights later.

How splits work technically

Your distributor collects 100% of streaming royalties, then divides them according to percentages you set — automatically paying each collaborator their share. No more "I'll pay you back when the money comes in."

What each distributor charges for splits

  • Symphonic: SplitShare is free — genuinely free splits.
  • DistroKid: "Splits" feature available; collaborators get paid automatically.
  • Amuse: 15% fee on splits when collaborators aren't Amuse subscribers. A collaboration can quietly cost you.
  • TuneCore: splits available, but collaborators must have their own paid accounts.

The conversation to have before release

  1. Agree percentages in writing. A WhatsApp message counts — but a simple split sheet signed by everyone is better. Decide: who gets what % of master royalties and what % of publishing.
  2. Separate master from publishing. The singer, producer, and beat-maker split the master; the lyricist and composer split the publishing. These are different pies.
  3. Decide who administers. One person usually uploads and manages the release — agree whether they get an admin fee.
  4. Plan for the future. What if someone wants out? What if a label wants to buy the track? Write it down now while everyone likes each other.

Common split mistakes

  • "We'll figure it out later" — the #1 cause of collaboration breakups.
  • Forgetting the producer — beat-makers own part of the master by default in most collaborations.
  • Verbal only — memories differ; screenshots don't.
  • Ignoring publishing — the songwriter's share is separate and often worth more long-term.
