AI Music Is Flooding Streaming Platforms — And the Industry Is Fighting Back

By Aabid Khan · October 14, 2026

AI Music Is Flooding Streaming Platforms — And the Industry Is Fighting Back

Open any streaming app in 2026 and you are listening to more AI-generated music than you realise. The numbers are staggering: on Deezer, fully AI-generated tracks hit nearly 75,000 uploads per day in April 2026 — roughly 44% of all daily uploads — and passed 50% of new uploads at the June peak. Yet those tracks account for just 1–3% of actual streams, and up to 85% of those streams were found to be fraudulent.

The flood is in uploads, not listening

That gap tells the real story. AI tools like Suno and Udio have made it possible to generate a finished-sounding track in minutes, and the upload pipelines are drowning in synthetic content. In 2025, 120.5 million of 253 million audio tracks on streaming services had ten streams or fewer — almost half the catalogue is barely heard. A growing share of that unheard supply was never made by a person.

Labels are deleting AI tracks by the hundreds of thousands

Sony Music asked streaming platforms to delete 260,000 AI-generated songs between early 2026 and the end of September — nearly double the 135,000 it had flagged by March. The problem is not just volume: it is AI-generated deepfake tracks imitating real artists' voices without permission, from Adele and Harry Styles to Britney Spears, Queen and Michael Jackson. Sony has said these tracks damage artists, mislead fans, and undermine ethical AI use.

Streaming fraud is now a criminal matter

The US Copyright Office has opened a formal inquiry into music streaming fraud, naming the dilution of the royalty pool as the primary harm. Comments are due November 23, 2026. Meanwhile, a man behind an $8 million AI-assisted streaming-fraud scheme received an 18-month prison sentence — a signal that fake streams are no longer treated as a victimless shortcut.

Industry estimates put streaming fraud at up to $2.2 billion per year. The mechanism is simple: bots repeatedly play AI-made songs, generating fake royalties while diluting the pool for real artists.

What platforms are doing

Deezer has commercialised its AI detection system and demonetised fraudulent AI streams. Spotify is rolling out an "AI Persona" badge for artists identified as AI-generated, and those acts are being removed from personalised recommendations. Luminate plans to add AI labels to tracks and artist profiles.

On the legal side, the US Copyright Office has ruled that fully AI-generated works cannot receive copyright protection, while compositions partly made with AI tools can still be registered.

What this means for real artists

The flood makes one thing more valuable than ever: a genuine human sound. As CITY JAM founder Pat Villaceran put it, speed is no longer the hard part — the real asset is the artist's own sound. AI can serve an original voice, but it cannot replace one.
