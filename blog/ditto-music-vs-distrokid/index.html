By Aabid Khan · October 14, 2026

Ditto Music vs DistroKid: The Budget Showdown

Ditto Music and DistroKid make almost identical headline promises: pay annually, release unlimited music, keep 100% of your ordinary streaming royalties. The difference is in the details — price, bundled tools, and what costs extra. For artists choosing between the two cheapest unlimited distributors, those details decide it.

Pricing: $19 vs $24.99 per year

Ditto's Starter plan costs $19 per year for one artist with unlimited releases and 100% royalty retention. DistroKid's Musician plan costs $24.99 per year for the same core deal. Ditto's Pro plan costs $59 per year and adds publishing, sync, YouTube Content ID, and capacity for additional artists. The $5.99 yearly gap between the entry plans is small, so the decision should rest on features, not the sticker price.

What is bundled, and what costs extra?

Ditto bundles several tools into Starter at no extra charge: free pre-save links, analytics, fan data, and automatic royalty splitting. It also includes release-date choice and publishing royalty collection. DistroKid charges separately for several of these: YouTube Content ID is a paid add-on per song per year with a 20% commission on the revenue it collects, and keeping your music live after cancelling costs $29 per release through Leave a Legacy. If you need Content ID and publishing collection, Ditto's bundled model can work out cheaper in practice.

Speed and reach

Both services deliver to 150+ platforms, and both report delivery to Spotify in roughly 1 to 3 days. Ditto highlights distribution beyond the usual Western stores, including services across China, Korea, South America, and Africa. DistroKid counters with brand recognition, a long-refined DIY workflow, and a large library of optional extras. For most independent artists, delivery speed and store coverage are effectively a tie.

Support and workflow

DistroKid is the more established platform with years of product refinement, but its base-plan support leans heavily on automation. Ditto positions itself as a career-development partner, offering promotional campaigns for upcoming talent and a label-services arm for artists it scouts. Neither offers the hands-on management of a curated distributor, so expect to drive your own marketing either way.

The verdict

Ditto wins on bundled value: more included for less money, especially for artists who need Content ID, splits, and publishing collection. DistroKid wins on ecosystem maturity and workflow polish. If your priority is the lowest total cost for a complete setup, Ditto edges it. If you already know DistroKid's workflow and it works for you, there is no urgent reason to switch.
