What Is a Licensing Deal vs a Record Deal?

By Aabid Khan · October 14, 2026

What Is a Licensing Deal vs a Record Deal?

Artists often use "record deal" and "licensing deal" as if they mean the same thing. They do not. The difference comes down to one question: who owns your masters? Understanding the distinction can save your career — and your catalogue.

The Traditional Record Deal

In a traditional record deal, the label funds your recording, owns the recordings, and pays you a royalty. Record contracts almost always provide that the artist grants and assigns the sound recording copyright — known as the "master" right — to the record company. This assignment typically lasts the full length of the statutory copyright term. Because the label owns the master, it negotiates and collects licensing fees and royalties from that ownership. In return, the artist gets funding, infrastructure, and the label's full machinery. This structure suits artists who want maximum investment and are comfortable trading ownership for it.

The Licensing Deal

In a licensing deal, you keep ownership of your masters — the label borrows the rights for a set period, often for specific territories. The label gets to release, market, and monetise your music during the licence term, and when the term ends, the rights revert to you. Artists who want label support without a permanent ownership transfer increasingly prefer this model. If a label offers you a traditional deal, it is always worth asking whether a licensing or distribution structure is on the table — especially if you walk in with real momentum, such as streaming numbers and an engaged audience.

The Distribution Deal

A third option is the distribution deal. Here, you keep full ownership and simply pay a distribution fee or revenue share for getting your music onto streaming platforms and into stores. This suits artists with an existing audience who want to stay fully independent. Independent artists who self-release keep full control of their masters — and some established artists have even re-recorded their music to regain ownership they signed away.

Key Differences at a Glance

  • Ownership: Traditional deal — usually the label. Licensing deal — you, with rights reverting after the term. Distribution deal — always you.
  • Funding: Traditional deals usually include advances and recording budgets. Licensing and distribution deals usually offer less upfront funding, or none.
  • Control: Licensing and distribution deals typically give the artist more say over releases, branding, and strategy.
  • Term: Traditional deals can lock in an artist for years or multiple albums. Licensing deals run for a defined period.

Which Should You Choose?

There is no single right answer. A traditional deal makes sense if you need major funding and infrastructure. A licensing deal makes sense if you have already built momentum and want support without giving up your catalogue. A distribution deal makes sense if you want to stay independent and just need reach. Whatever you choose, have a lawyer review the contract, and pay special attention to reversion rights — the clause that decides when, if ever, your music comes home.
