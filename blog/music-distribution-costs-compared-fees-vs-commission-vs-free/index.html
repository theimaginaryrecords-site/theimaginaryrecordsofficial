Music Distribution Costs Compared: Yearly Fees vs Commission vs Free

By Aabid Khan · October 14, 2026

Music Distribution Costs Compared: Yearly Fees vs Commission vs Free

Every distributor charges you in one of three ways: a yearly fee, a commission on your earnings, or a one-time fee per release. Each model wins for a different kind of artist — and each has hidden costs that the headline price does not show. Here is the honest comparison.

Model 1: Flat yearly fee, 100% royalties

DistroKid charges $24.99 per year for unlimited uploads with 100% of ordinary royalties. Ditto Music charges $19 per year for the same core deal, and Amuse charges $23.99 per year. You pay the same whether you release one song or fifty, so the cost per release falls to almost nothing if you release often. The catch is the add-ons: DistroKid charges extra for YouTube Content ID, cover song licensing, and catalogue permanence, and your music can be removed if the subscription lapses. This model wins for prolific artists earning meaningful revenue.

Model 2: One-time fee per release, permanent commission

CD Baby charges $9.99 per single and $14.99 per album as a one-time fee, with no annual renewal — but it keeps a permanent 9% commission on your royalties. There is no subscription to lapse and no removal risk. The trade-off compounds over time: on $100,000 of lifetime earnings, that 9% costs $9,000. This model wins for artists who release rarely and want their catalogue to stay live forever without thinking about renewals.

Model 3: Free upfront, revenue share

RouteNote's Free tier costs nothing to upload and pays you 85% of revenue, keeping 15%. There is no upfront cost and no subscription. The trade-off is obvious: the more you earn, the more you give away. Imagine two artists — one earning $100,000 a year, one earning $10. The high earner would rather pay a fixed subscription and keep virtually everything. The beginner may prefer to pay nothing and share a percentage only if something is earned. This model wins for artists just starting out.

Hidden costs to check before you sign up

Add-on services are where the real cost hides. YouTube Content ID can cost a per-song annual fee plus a 20% commission on the revenue it collects. Withdrawal charges vary by payment method — DistroKid's processor charges $5.62 for non-US local bank and SEPA withdrawals, while international wires cost more. Cover song licensing, publishing administration, and catalogue permanence add-ons all add up. And check the cancellation policy: DistroKid may remove your catalogue, TuneCore takes releases down 42 days after a missed renewal, while Too Lost and LANDR keep releases earning at 85% and Amuse keeps them live at a 25% commission.

Run your own numbers

For unlimited releases, the cheapest yearly plans are around $19 per year. For a single release, compare a one-time $9.99 fee plus 9% forever against $19 to $24.99 per year at 100%. The right answer depends on how many songs you release and how much they earn — not on which headline price looks lowest.
