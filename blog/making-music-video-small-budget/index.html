Making a Music Video on a Small Budget

By Aabid Khan · October 14, 2026

Making a Music Video on a Small Budget

A great music video does not require a big budget — it requires a strong idea, careful planning, and resourcefulness. Some of the most iconic videos ever made were shot guerrilla-style on minimal budgets. Here is how to make yours count.

Concept beats budget

A strong concept makes up for limited resources. A simple idea executed creatively beats an expensive idea executed badly. Before spending anything, develop your concept: one clear visual idea, a storyboard of each scene, and a plan for how the video serves the song. Keep it simple — one location and a small cast will stretch your budget far further than five locations and a crowd.

Shoot with what you have

A modern smartphone shoots video good enough for a professional-looking result, especially with natural light. Shoot during the times of day when natural light is best, and use free locations — streets, parks, a friend's apartment — that do not require permits or fees. Keep your crew small and multitask: a director who also operates the camera, friends and family as cast and helpers.

One-take and performance videos save money

Shooting in one continuous take saves enormously on editing time and shoot days. Performance videos — a stripped-back acoustic version or a live take — are another budget-friendly format that can make a big impact with minimal equipment. Lyric videos are also a valid low-cost option, and can be made with free or affordable editing tools.

Edit with free professional tools

Free editors like DaVinci Resolve, Shotcut, and Lightworks offer professional-grade editing without the price tag. Learn the basics well — cutting on the beat, clean transitions, simple colour grading — before reaching for fancy effects. Good editing is invisible; bad editing distracts from the song.

Trade skills, don't just spend money

Exchange favours with people who have the skills you need: offer a track in exchange for video help, or collaborate with a filmmaker building their portfolio. Skill swaps get you professional help at no cash cost — and build relationships that last beyond one video.

One video can become many pieces of content. Cut a vertical teaser for Reels and Shorts, a behind-the-scenes clip for Stories, and a short hook for TikTok. Short-form clips often reach more new listeners than the full video itself, so plan your shoot to capture vertical-friendly moments alongside the main footage.
