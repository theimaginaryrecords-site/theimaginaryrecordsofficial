Mastering Explained: The Final Polish on Your Song

By Aabid Khan · October 14, 2026

Mastering Explained: The Final Polish on Your Song

Mastering is the final stage of music production. It takes your finished mix and prepares it for release — refining its overall sound, loudness, and consistency so it plays well everywhere, from phone speakers to club systems to car stereos.

Mastering works on the whole song

Unlike mixing, which balances individual tracks, mastering works with the final stereo file. The mastering engineer evaluates the mix as a finished object and applies controlled processing to improve its stability, consistency, and loudness without altering the song's intent.

What mastering actually does

A mastering session typically covers tonal balance (final EQ adjustments), overall dynamics, level management, and quality control. The engineer also prepares the correct export formats for distribution — streaming platforms have their own technical requirements, and a good master meets them.

Loudness: controlled, not crushed

One of mastering's most misunderstood jobs is loudness. The goal is not to make your song as loud as physically possible — over-limiting destroys dynamics and makes music tiring to listen to. A good master is competitive in loudness while keeping the song's punch and emotion intact.

Consistency across a release

For an EP or album, mastering also ensures every track feels like part of the same release — similar loudness, similar tonal character, and sensible gaps and sequencing between songs. A collection of individually loud tracks that jump in volume between songs feels amateur; a mastered release feels unified.

The hard truth: mastering cannot fix a bad mix

Mastering can improve a strong mix. It cannot rebuild a weak one. If your mix has serious balance problems, no amount of mastering will fix them — the mix needs to be revisited first. That is why mixing and mastering are separate jobs, and why most releases need both. When in doubt, fix the mix before you pay for a master. A great master starts with a great mix, and no plugin can replace that foundation.

Streaming platforms normalise playback loudness, so a master that is simply louder does not sound louder to listeners — it just gets turned down. What matters is how your master sounds after normalisation: clean, dynamic, and balanced. Deliver high-quality WAV files to your distributor and let the platform handle playback levels. Check your distributor's current delivery specs before exporting your final master.
