ISRC Code in India: What It Is and How to Get One (2026)

Every song you've ever streamed has a hidden serial number attached to it. That number is the ISRC — and if you're releasing music in India in 2026, you need to understand what it does, because it's the difference between getting paid and getting lost.

What an ISRC is

ISRC stands for International Standard Recording Code. It's a 12-character alphanumeric code that uniquely identifies one specific recording of a song — not the song itself, but that particular version. A studio version, a live version, and a slowed-and-reverb edit each get their own ISRC.

The code has four parts: a 2-letter country code (IN for India), a 3-character registrant code identifying who issued it, a 2-digit year, and a 5-digit designation number. It looks like this: IN-TIR-26-00001.

Why it matters more than you think

ISRCs are how the entire royalty system tracks your music. When your song is streamed on Spotify, played on radio, or used in a YouTube video, the ISRC is what connects that play back to you. No ISRC means:

  • Your streams may not be attributed to you correctly.
  • Royalty societies and platforms can't match plays to your catalog.
  • Content ID and chart tracking won't work properly.

Every legitimate distributor assigns ISRCs to your releases. If a "distributor" doesn't — run.

ISRC vs. UPC: don't confuse them

Artists often mix these up. The ISRC identifies an individual track. The UPC (barcode) identifies the release — the single, EP or album as a product. A single needs one ISRC and one UPC. An album with 10 tracks needs 10 ISRCs and one UPC. Both are free with proper distribution.

How to get ISRC codes in India

You have two routes:

  • Through your distributor (recommended): This is how 99% of independent artists get ISRCs. Your distributor is a registered ISRC manager and assigns codes automatically with each release, at no cost. The codes are registered in the global database and work on every platform worldwide.
  • Directly from the national agency: India's ISRC allocation is administered through the national agency (PPL India handles registrant codes). This route makes sense mainly for large labels managing thousands of codes; for an independent artist it's unnecessary paperwork.

Never reuse an ISRC from an old release for a new recording, and never let two distributors assign different ISRCs to the same track — duplicate codes create royalty conflicts that can take months to untangle. Pick one distributor per release and let them handle it.
