EDM in India: How Electronic Music Found Its Audience

When Sunburn launched in 2007 in the coastal town of Candolim, Goa, electronic dance music was still an underground curiosity in India. Less than two decades later, Sunburn is described as Asia's largest electronic music festival, drawing millions of fans to events in Goa, Pune and Mumbai — and in December 2026 it staged its first international edition in Abu Dhabi. EDM went from niche to mainstream in India faster than almost any other genre.

Why India took to EDM

EDM had a natural advantage in India: it has no lyrics to translate. As Sunburn's founder Shailendra Singh put it, dance music has no language barrier, so everyone can understand it. The music is built for a shared physical experience — massive sound systems, lights, lasers and crowds moving together — which made it a natural fit for India's festival culture and its young, experience-hungry audience.

The festival engine

Sunburn's growth tracked the rise of the scene. After starting in Goa, the festival relocated to Pune in 2016 for larger audiences and bigger production, then expanded with touring editions across India. Its 2026 Abu Dhabi edition, produced with BookMyShow and Ethara and headlined by Martin Garrix and Skrillex, placed the Indian-born brand alongside global giants like Tomorrowland and Ultra. The flagship Indian edition was scheduled for Mumbai's Mahalaxmi Racecourse on December 18 and 19, 2026.

What it means for Indian artists

Festivals like Sunburn gave Indian DJs and producers a stage they never had before, alongside the biggest international names. The scene also proved that India could support large-scale ticketed live music beyond Bollywood. For independent artists, the lesson is clear: a genre that once seemed foreign can become a national movement when live experiences carry it.

EDM's early proof in India

The scene's commercial potential showed early. Forbes India reported that a Swedish House Mafia concert in Mumbai sold some 18,000 tickets at an average of Rs 4,000, while Sunburn Arena events with acts like DJ Tiesto, Armin van Buuren and Swedish House Mafia sold thousands of VIP tickets. At the time, seven of India's ten biggest music concerts were EDM gigs. Beyond Sunburn, boutique festivals like Magnetic Fields in Alsisar, Rajasthan, and Echoes of Earth in Bangalore proved electronic music could thrive in heritage settings and green, sustainability-focused formats too.
