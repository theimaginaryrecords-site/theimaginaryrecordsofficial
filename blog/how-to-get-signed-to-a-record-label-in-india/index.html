How to Get Signed to a Record Label in India

By Aabid Khan · October 14, 2026

How to Get Signed to a Record Label in India

Getting signed is not about luck. It is about sending the right music, to the right label, in the right way — after building enough momentum that the label can see your potential. Here is a practical roadmap for Indian artists.

Step 1: Build Before You Pitch

Labels sign momentum, not just talent. Before approaching any label, release music independently, grow your streaming numbers, play live shows, and build an engaged audience on social media. A label wants to see proof that people already care about your music. Artists with real traction — growing monthly listeners, a viral moment, press coverage, or a strong live following — get taken far more seriously than artists with only unreleased demos.

Step 2: Research Labels That Fit Your Sound

Not every label is right for every artist. Study rosters: a label that releases Bollywood soundtracks may not be the home for your lo-fi EP. Identify 10–15 labels that genuinely match your genre and artist profile. Listen to their recent releases, check their submission guidelines, and understand what they look for. A tailored approach beats mass-blasting every label with the same email.

Step 3: Prepare a Professional Demo Package

Your submission should include three to five of your strongest tracks — not a full album, and never rough mixes. Use high-quality audio files with clean metadata and professional file names. Add a one-page press kit: a concise biography, high-resolution photos, streaming statistics, notable achievements, and clear contact information. Keep your pitch short and specific: who you are, what you sound like, what you have achieved, and why you fit that label.

Step 4: Submit Properly — and Follow Up Once

Follow each label's submission process exactly, whether that means a form, an email, or a demo platform. Do not attach large files unless requested, and do not mass-email multiple labels in one thread. Then wait: labels receive hundreds of submissions, so allow four to six weeks before a polite follow-up. Limit yourself to a maximum of two follow-up attempts — if you hear nothing after the second, take no response as your answer and move on.

Step 5: Read the Deal Before You Celebrate

An offer is the beginning of negotiations, not the end. Have a music lawyer review anything beyond a simple single or EP licence before you sign. Understand master versus publishing rights, the length of the term, and what the label is actually committing to deliver. The best deal is not always the biggest name — it is the partner that fits your music, your goals, and your career.
