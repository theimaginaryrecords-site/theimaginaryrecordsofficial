By Aabid Khan · October 14, 2026

Billie Eilish: The Bedroom Sessions That Redefined Pop

Billie Eilish Pirate Baird O'Connell, born December 18, 2001, in Los Angeles, California, is an American singer and songwriter who became one of the defining artists of her generation without ever following the traditional pop playbook. Her whispered vocals, horror-tinged visuals, and songs recorded at home with her brother Finneas changed what a global pop star could sound like.

Ocean Eyes and the Viral Beginning

Eilish first gained public attention in 2015 with her debut single "Ocean Eyes," written and produced by her brother Finneas O'Connell. Originally uploaded to SoundCloud, the song spread quickly and drew industry attention. Her debut extended play, Don't Smile at Me (2017), was commercially successful and charted within the top 15 in the US, UK, Canada, and Australia.

The Debut Album and the Grammy Sweep

Her first studio album, When We All Fall Asleep, Where Do We Go? (2019), debuted atop the US Billboard 200 and reached number one in the UK, powered by her first Billboard Hot 100 number one, "Bad Guy." At the 2020 Grammy Awards, she became the youngest artist in Grammy history to win all four general-field categories — Best New Artist, Record of the Year, Song of the Year, and Album of the Year — in the same year.

Film Songs and Artistic Growth

In 2020, she co-wrote and performed "No Time to Die," the theme song for the James Bond film of the same name, which topped the UK singles chart and later won the Academy Award for Best Original Song. Her second album, Happier Than Ever (2021), reached number one in 25 countries. Her song "What Was I Made For?" for the Barbie film (2023) earned her a second Oscar for Best Original Song in 2024. Her third album, Hit Me Hard and Soft, arrived in 2024, featuring "Birds of a Feather" among its standout tracks. She was named to Time magazine's inaugural Time 100 Next list in 2019 and made it into the main Time 100 list in 2021.

Legacy

Working extensively with Finneas from their family home and later professional studios, Eilish combined intimate songwriting with unconventional production. She proved that quiet, strange, deeply personal music could fill stadiums — and that a new generation of artists could build careers on their own terms.
