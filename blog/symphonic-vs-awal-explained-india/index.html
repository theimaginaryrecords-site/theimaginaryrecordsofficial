Symphonic vs AWAL: The "Label-Like" Distributors Explained for Indian Artists

Symphonic and AWAL aren't self-serve like DistroKid — they're curated, application-based, and work more like label partners. Here's what that actually means and whether you should apply.

AWAL: free, but 90% get rejected

AWAL charges no upfront fee — instead it takes 15% commission (you keep 85%). The catch: roughly 90% of applications are rejected. AWAL curates its roster like a label A&R team. If accepted to Core, you get distribution plus playlist pitching muscle backed by Sony Music (which owns AWAL). No dedicated rep on the Core tier, $45 payout threshold, 30-day exit notice. Best for: artists with traction who want label-grade support without signing a deal.

Symphonic: $29.99/year Starter, invite-only Partner tier

Symphonic's Starter plan ($29.99/year, raised May 2026) gives 100% of streaming royalties — but only 70% of UGC revenue (TikTok, YouTube, Facebook, Instagram). It offers free royalty splits (SplitShare), sync licensing opportunities, and royalty advances up to $5M through Symphonic NEXT. The Partner tier is custom-priced and invite-only. Watch out: releases come down if you don't renew, and you can't move your catalog until your contract term ends.

Head to head

  • Cost: AWAL free (15% commission) vs Symphonic $29.99/yr
  • Selectivity: AWAL rejects ~90%; Symphonic Starter is open signup, Partner is invite-only
  • YouTube/UGC: Symphonic keeps 30% of UGC revenue — significant for Indian artists where YouTube dominates
  • Lock-in: Symphonic restricts catalog moves during the term; AWAL has a 30-day exit
  • Advances: both offer them (Symphonic NEXT up to $5M), but advances are recoupable loans, not free money

Should Indian artists apply?

If you have real streaming traction (hundreds of thousands of streams) and want playlist/marketing muscle, AWAL is worth the application — it's free to try. Symphonic suits artists who want splits, sync, and advances in one place. For everyone else — especially artists early in their journey — an open distributor with no gatekeeping (like TIR) gets your music out now instead of waiting on an application.
