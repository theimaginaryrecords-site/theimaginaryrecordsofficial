When Do Music Distributors Pay Royalties in India? (2026)

"My song has 50,000 streams — where's my money?" It's the most common question new artists ask, and the answer surprises most of them: streaming royalties take months to reach you. Not because anyone is holding your money hostage, but because of how the reporting chain works. Here's the honest timeline.

The royalty journey: stream to bank account

When someone streams your song on Spotify in January, here's what happens:

  • January–February: Spotify counts the streams and calculates payouts per rights holder.
  • March–April: Spotify reports the earnings to your distributor (platforms typically report 2–3 months after the streaming month).
  • April–May: Your distributor processes the report, applies your royalty split, and adds it to your balance.
  • May–June: Payout, depending on your distributor's schedule.

Total: roughly 4–5 months from stream to money in your account. This is industry-standard — it works the same way for DistroKid, TuneCore, and every other distributor. Anyone promising instant streaming payouts is misleading you.

How often do distributors pay?

Payout frequency varies:

  • Monthly: Some distributors pay every month once your balance crosses a minimum threshold (often ₹500–₹1,000).
  • Quarterly: Others pay four times a year. Slower, but the same money arrives.

What matters more than frequency is transparency — can you see your per-platform, per-track earnings in a dashboard, or are you just handed a number? Ask this before choosing a distributor.

How Indian artists actually receive the money

For Indian artists, the practical details matter:

  • Bank transfer (NEFT/IMPS): The cleanest route. TIR pays directly to Indian bank accounts in rupees — no intermediary, no conversion loss.
  • PayPal: Common with US distributors, but PayPal's currency conversion and withdrawal fees eat 3–5% of your payout, and Indian PayPal accounts have withdrawal quirks.
  • Minimum thresholds: Most distributors won't process a ₹40 payout — the transfer costs more than the payment. Thresholds of ₹500–₹1,000 are normal.

What about YouTube and Content ID revenue?

YouTube pays on its own cycle — roughly 2 months after the viewing month — and Content ID earnings flow through your distributor after that. Expect YouTube money to lag about the same 4–5 months. The good news: it keeps compounding. A song that finds an audience in lyric videos and edits generates claims for years.

Red flags to watch for

  • A distributor that can't show you per-stream, per-platform breakdowns.
  • "Processing fees" or "withdrawal fees" deducted from your royalties beyond the stated split.
  • No clear payout schedule published anywhere.
  • Requiring you to chase them for every payment.

The bottom line

Plan your finances around a 4–5 month lag and you'll never be disappointed. Release consistently, watch your dashboard grow, and treat payouts as a trailing indicator of work you did months ago. With TIR, you keep 85% of everything — and you can see exactly where every rupee came from.
