Everything Indian independent artists ask us about music distribution — answered honestly. Can't find your answer? Talk to us.

Getting Started

Music distribution is how your song gets from your studio to Spotify, Apple Music, JioSaavn, Gaana, YouTube Music and 200+ other platforms worldwide. A distributor delivers your audio and metadata to the stores, collects the royalties your streams earn, and pays them to you. You cannot upload directly to Spotify or JioSaavn as an artist — you need a distributor in between.

Getting Started

It depends on the distributor. Some charge a yearly fee per release, some take a percentage of your royalties, and some do both. The Imaginary Records charges no joining fee — you keep up to 85% of the royalties your music earns, and we retain 15% as a service fee.

Getting Started

Usually there is a trade-off. Some "free" distributors take a large cut of your royalties, charge for basic features like Content ID or fast delivery, or make it hard to leave. Always read the terms before uploading. With TIR there is no joining fee and no hidden charges — the split is a flat 85% to you.

Releasing Music

Once your release passes our quality check, delivery to stores takes 24–48 hours. Spotify and most major platforms then need a few days to process and publish it. In practice, submit your release at least 1–2 weeks before your planned release date so everything is live on time.

Releasing Music

Indian platforms like JioSaavn, Gaana, Wynk and Hungama generally go live within the same window as global stores — a few days after delivery. Timelines vary slightly by platform and by how clean your metadata is, which is why we review every release before sending it out.

Releasing Music

Upload a WAV file, 44.1 kHz / 16-bit minimum (24-bit preferred), with your final master — no clipping, no MP3s. Stores transcode everything themselves, so give them the highest quality source you have. Artwork must be a square JPG or PNG, at least 3000×3000 pixels, with no blurry text or social media handles on it.

Releasing Music

Cover songs need a licence from the original songwriter or publisher before they can be distributed — releasing one without permission can get your release taken down and your account penalised. If you have the proper licence, we can distribute it. If you're unsure, ask us before uploading.

Releasing Music

Royalties & Payouts

There is no fixed rate — it varies by the listener's plan, country and month. In India, independent artists typically see roughly ₹0.12 to ₹0.25 per stream. One lakh (100,000) Indian streams therefore earns somewhere around ₹12,000–₹25,000 before your distributor's share. Anyone promising an exact per-stream rate is guessing.

Rights & Technical

An ISRC is a unique code assigned to each individual track, and a UPC (barcode) identifies the whole release. Stores require them to track your streams and sales. You don't need to buy them yourself — we assign ISRC and UPC codes to every release free of charge.

Rights & Technical

Content ID is YouTube's system that detects when your music is used in other people's videos and lets you earn ad revenue from those uses instead of just blocking them. If someone uploads a video with your song in the background, Content ID can claim it on your behalf and the ad money comes to you through your distributor.
